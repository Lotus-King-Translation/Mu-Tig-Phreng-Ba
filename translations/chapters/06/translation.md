The holder of the vajra again petitioned:[^T06-001]

<!-- pair: MTP-000323 -->

“O, O great Vajradhara, Lord of Secret Mantra!
The oneness of all buddhas—
what is that very enlightened intent like?[^T06-001][^T06-002]

<!-- pair: MTP-000324 -->

Through the stains of the five afflictions,
how do sentient beings become bound?
Bhagavān, speak to me.”[^T06-001]

<!-- pair: MTP-000325 -->

The Bhagavān spoke:[^T06-001]

<!-- pair: MTP-000326 -->

“As for the single truth of all buddhas,
intrinsic nature, through essence,
is distinguished from that itself.[^T06-003]

<!-- pair: MTP-000327 -->

In the appearance of essence, primordial knowing,
intrinsic nature pervades all, endowed with five embodiments.
The result is the unique sphere.[^T06-003]

<!-- pair: MTP-000328 -->

In appearance itself there is union;[^T06-004]

<!-- pair: MTP-000329 -->

[Printed page 481.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000330 -->

in emptiness there is clear light.[^T06-004]

<!-- pair: MTP-000331 -->

Essence itself is simultaneous;
within intrinsic nature itself, [it is] gradual.[^T06-004]

<!-- pair: MTP-000332 -->

In the all-pervading, empty nature of phenomena,
there is freedom from the dust of extremes.
In the appearance of intrinsic nature, clear light,
there is freedom from the stain of delusion.[^T06-005]

<!-- pair: MTP-000333 -->

In dharma embodiment, clear light, great bliss,
the extremes of entities are empty.
In the naturally arising king of awareness,
[it is] beyond the domains of high and low.[^T06-005]

<!-- pair: MTP-000334 -->

I myself, from the enlightened intent of buddhas,

<!-- pair: MTP-000335 -->

I am the lower destinies, hells and the like.

I am the precious ones, buddhas and the like.[^T06-006]

<!-- pair: MTP-000336 -->

[Printed page 482.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000337 -->

I am the teacher, retinue, and teaching.
I am gathering—gathering my own retinue.
I am view, cultivation, and activity.
I am empowerment, commitments, and vows.[^T06-007]

<!-- pair: MTP-000338 -->

I am offerings, meditative stability, and deep absorption.
I am washing, cleanliness, and stainlessness.
I am virtuous and nonvirtuous activity.
I am sūtra discourses, discipline, and higher doctrine.[^T06-008]

<!-- pair: MTP-000339 -->

I am the two truths, empty and not empty.
I am the general and the distinctions of the vehicles.
I am wrongdoing, action, and ripening.
I am the world, its receptacle and contents.[^T06-009]

<!-- pair: MTP-000340 -->

Therefore I am what goes and what does not go.
I am action and that itself, the stain of affliction.
I am the faculties and objects.[^T06-009]

<!-- pair: MTP-000341 -->

[Printed page 483.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000342 -->

I am the aggregates, elements, and sense bases.
I am flesh, blood, and bone.
I am names, letters, words, and forms.
I am apprehended object and apprehending subject, objects, and conceptualizing a self.[^T06-010]

<!-- pair: MTP-000343 -->

I am suffering, ignorance, and delusion.
I am sound, smell, taste, and touch.
I am the mistaken apprehension of heat and cold.
I am hunger, thirst, and bewilderment.
I am pleasure, suffering, and equanimity.[^T06-011]

<!-- pair: MTP-000344 -->

In brief, I am however [things] appear.
I am undefeated, victorious over all.
I have no contrived utterance; I am exalted beyond all.
I have neither permanence nor annihilation; I enter in every manner.[^T06-012]

<!-- pair: MTP-000345 -->

[Printed page 484.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000346 -->

I have no object of seeing; I appear manifestly to all.
I am complete to the depths of everything; I am greater than all.
I have no path to traverse; I have passed beyond all.
I am unique; I am related to all.[^T06-013]

<!-- pair: MTP-000347 -->

I have no ground on which to abide; I depend on all.
I have no rival to compete with; I am unique among all.
For me, insentient and aware are not two; I pervade all.
I wear the clothing of darkness; I show appearance to all.[^T06-013]

<!-- pair: MTP-000348 -->

Having made a རྒྱ་ཐེག of mountains, my strength is great.
Through ཧུབ་ཀྱིས་སྟོབ་པས with the ocean, my ལྒོབ is great.
Having put a mountain into a sesame seed, my deeds are great.[^T06-014]

<!-- pair: MTP-000349 -->

[Printed page 485.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000350 -->

Having worn darkness as clothing, I am a great hero.
Having traversed saṃsāra and nirvāṇa in one stride, my leap is great.
Having pressed down the grounds at one time, my majesty is great.
Having spread out the sun and moon as a seat, my splendor is great.[^T06-015]

<!-- pair: MTP-000351 -->

Because saṃsāra and nirvāṇa are complete in me at one time, I am free from doing.
Because appearance arises unobstructedly in me, [it is] self-arising itself.
Because realization and non-realization are absent in me, [I am] primordially buddha.
Because hope and fear are absent in me, saṃsāra and nirvāṇa are complete at one time.[^T06-015]

<!-- pair: MTP-000352 -->

[Printed page 486.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000353 -->

Because ཡེ་ང and apprehension are absent in me, I am free from all fear.
Because there is no master of contriving in me, [there is] letting be from the beginning.
Because apprehended object and apprehending subject are not objects in me, [there is] self-abiding from the beginning.
Because receptacle and contents are not two in me, everything is myself.
Because bearers of phenomena and the nature of phenomena are absent in me, everything is one.”[^T06-016]

<!-- pair: MTP-000354 -->

The holder of the vajra again petitioned:[^T06-001]

<!-- pair: MTP-000355 -->

“If, in this way, all worldly phenomena of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa
are one in essence,
[—] is bound by sentient beings?[^T06-017]

<!-- pair: MTP-000356 -->

or is that itself unbound?
Or is [it] bound by others?
Bhagavān, སུངས to me.”[^T06-017]

<!-- pair: MTP-000357 -->

The Bhagavān spoke:[^T06-001]

<!-- pair: MTP-000358 -->

“As for sentient beings’ five afflictions,[^T06-018]

<!-- pair: MTP-000360 -->

[they] are accompanied by adventitious conditions.

[it] is called the state of inseparable oneness.[^T06-018]

<!-- pair: MTP-000361 -->

Likewise, [there is] no stain of affliction.
If naturally arising intrinsic nature itself is known,
how could sentient beings be bound?[^T06-018]

<!-- pair: MTP-000362 -->

If that itself is held as true,
even buddhas themselves become bound;
why would sentient beings not be bound?[^T06-019]

<!-- pair: MTP-000363 -->

If [one] knows that there is no truth and no intrinsic nature,
the stains are purified in their own place.
Like turbidity in water becoming clear by itself,
all phenomena are one in lacking intrinsic nature.[^T06-019]

<!-- pair: MTP-000364 -->

If [one] does not know nonentity in this way,
and, regarding the aggregates, elements, and sense bases,
[one] holds through apprehended object and apprehending subject,[^T06-020]

<!-- pair: MTP-000365 -->

[Printed page 488.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000366 -->

[one] abides for a long time in saṃsāra itself.
In the house of the three realms,
[one] enters the prison of name and form,
bound with the iron of ignorance and action,[^T06-020]

<!-- pair: MTP-000367 -->

separated from the naturally arising lamp itself,
covered by the thick darkness of saṃsāra,
attached to the ལན་ཚའི་རོལ of desire,
bound tightly by the noose of deluded dullness,[^T06-021]

<!-- pair: MTP-000368 -->

tormented by the heat of the fire of hatred,
with the head wrapped in pride,
obscured by the door of jealousy itself,[^T06-021]

<!-- pair: MTP-000369 -->

surrounded by an army of anger and the like,
secured on right and left by mindfulness itself,

<!-- pair: MTP-000370 -->

[Printed page 489.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000371 -->

sinking into the mud of past and future habitual tendencies,
joining hands with ripening itself,
through action, mother and child, the intermediate state, and—
through action, having linked mother and child,[^T06-022]

<!-- pair: MTP-000372 -->

from one to another, like buckets of water,
[one] takes bodies in good and bad stages
and is born into the respective families.[^T06-022]

<!-- pair: MTP-000373 -->

Having འབོགས into generations of grasping at “I,”
[one] sinks into the depths of the ocean of suffering,
held at the heart by the hook of the lower destinies.
Oneself binds oneself: afflictions are the enemy.[^T06-023]

<!-- pair: MTP-000374 -->

For hell beings, [it] appears as fire and water;
for hungry ghosts, [as] hunger and thirst;
for animals, like ལན་བུན;[^T06-024]

<!-- pair: MTP-000375 -->

for humans, [as] aggregates, elements,
sense bases, and the five elements.
That too is pleasure, suffering, and equanimity.[^T06-024]

<!-- pair: MTP-000376 -->

For demigods, [it] appears as armor and weapons;
for gods, [as] the qualities of desire.[^T06-024]

<!-- pair: MTP-000377 -->

[Printed page 490.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000378 -->

Lord of Secrets, holder of the vajra!
Those sentient beings in saṃsāra,
although bound by their own differentiating conceptualization,
are certain to be liberated because [it] lacks intrinsic nature,
for, in the basic space of phenomena, [they] are even.[^T06-025]

<!-- pair: MTP-000379 -->

The five aggregates, elements, and sense bases
are liberated as families and lords of the families.
The bearers of phenomena of apprehended object and apprehending subject
are liberated as means and discerning knowing itself.[^T06-025]

<!-- pair: MTP-000380 -->

Saṃsāra is liberated into the basic space of phenomena.
The nature of phenomena is immeasurable, unimpeded penetration itself.
The ultimate is unfixed, greatly open.
[One] possesses the magical key of primordial knowing.[^T06-026]

<!-- pair: MTP-000381 -->

The lamp of clear light and emptiness is clear.
Awareness, self-appearance, is a great light.
[One] is not bound by attachment, like a snake’s knot.[^T06-026]

<!-- pair: MTP-000382 -->

[Printed page 491.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000383 -->

Attached to the flavor of bliss in experiential acquaintance,
[one’s] own body is bound by the noose of compassionate responsiveness.
The fire of discerning knowing burns the afflictions;
[one] also wears the clothing of great clear light.
Having opened the treasury door of the nature of phenomena—[^T06-027]

<!-- pair: MTP-000384 -->

an army’s force bears the weapon of self-awareness;
the body rests on the two, view and activity;
with the noose of conceptual thought, [one] is bound in saṃsāra.[^T06-027]

<!-- pair: MTP-000385 -->

The self-awareness of attachment appears before and after;
the hand is joined with phenomena free from conceptualization.
The two—mother and child of the nature of phenomena—meet.
Self-awareness, primordial knowing, is unique.[^T06-027]

<!-- pair: MTP-000386 -->

Having found the object of great emptiness and clarity,
[one] attains dharma embodiment without an apprehending subject,
goes beyond the excellence of continuous deep absorption,
and escapes from the expanse of the ocean of suffering.[^T06-028]

<!-- pair: MTP-000387 -->

The enlightened intent of the means of secret mantra is one;
primordial knowing is complete in primordial knowing itself.[^T06-028]

<!-- pair: MTP-000388 -->

[Printed page 492.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000389 -->

Because ignorance itself is completely purified,
[one] abides in dharma embodiment, great clear light.
Within the intrinsic nature of great bliss,
entities and nonentities have passed beyond sorrow.[^T06-029]

<!-- pair: MTP-000390 -->

Empty primordial knowing is empty in essence.
As for all-pervading intrinsic nature, primordial knowing,
sound, smell, and taste have passed beyond sorrow.[^T06-029]

<!-- pair: MTP-000391 -->

As for dharma embodiment without conceptual elaborations,
form and consciousness have passed beyond sorrow.
Because the primordial knowing of awareness pervades expansively,
the entire three-thousandfold [world] has passed beyond sorrow.[^T06-029]

<!-- pair: MTP-000392 -->

Because the basic space of phenomena is completely purified from the beginning,
the five elements themselves have passed beyond sorrow.
Because the single embodiment of buddhas pervades all,
names and letters have passed beyond sorrow.[^T06-029]

<!-- pair: MTP-000393 -->

[Printed page 493.]

<!-- pair: MTP-000394 -->

Through indestructible vajra primordial knowing,
earth, stones, mountains, and rocks have passed beyond sorrow.
In the nature of phenomena, free from conceptual elaborations,
conventional designation itself has passed beyond sorrow.[^T06-029]

<!-- pair: MTP-000395 -->

Emaho!
This, from the perfectly [awakened] buddhas’
very essence, has not moved:
the unchanging nature of phenomena, utterly stable.[^T06-030]

<!-- pair: MTP-000396 -->

Self-appearance, self-liberation, one’s own object;
intrinsic nature, self-purified, one’s own radiance;
leaving as it is—leave, leave in itself.”[^T06-030]

petitioned the Bhagavān in these words:[^T06-031]

“Alas, Bhagavān Vajradhara!
For all these worldly sentient beings,
what is the ground on which they abide like?
What is the path along which they go like?
What sort of result do they attain?
Vajradhara, speak to me.”[^T06-031]

[it] abides in an all-pervading manner.[^T06-032]

The embodiment of vajra—vajra chains—

<!-- pair: MTP-000405 -->
